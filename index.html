Microwave Magnonics Lab, Chungbuk National University

Hybrid magnonics at microwave frequencies

We couple spin waves (magnons) to phonons, microwave photons and superconducting circuits, and turn those couplings into devices for quantum transduction, quantum sensing and neuromorphic computing.

Led by Kyongmo An, formerly a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow at EPFL, a postdoctoral researcher at SPINTEC and a Sejong Science Fellow at KRISS.

Research

Magnons, the quanta of spin waves, carry information at gigahertz frequencies without moving electric charge. Their frequency tunes with a magnetic field, and they couple strongly to lattice vibrations (phonons) and to microwave photons.

M² Lab works where these three meet. We use phonons to couple distant magnets, connect magnons to superconducting circuits to convert quantum information between platforms, and exploit nonlinear spin-wave dynamics for signal processing and neuromorphic computing.

  • Magnon–phonon coupling

    Magnons and phonons hybridize strongly in magnetic garnets. We use acoustic phonons to transfer angular momentum coherently between distant magnets and to engineer bright and dark collective states.

    • Phys. Rev. X 12, 011060 (2022)
    • Phys. Rev. B 101, 060407(R) (2020)
    • Phys. Rev. Applied 20, 014046 (2023)

  • Quantum magnonics

    Magnons couple to microwave photons and superconducting qubits, which makes them candidates for quantum transducers and sensors. Under our Open-Lab project we are building a cryogenic platform for magnon–superconductor quantum transduction, including the generation of squeezed magnon states.

    • Phys. Rev. X 12, 011060 (2022)

  • Spin-wave signal processing and neuromorphic devices

    Spin waves carry information without charge currents, and their nonlinear interactions can perform logic and mimic neural functions. We study nonlinear magnonic waveguides at ultrahigh frequency resolution and design antennas that control spin-wave nonreciprocity.

    • Nat. Commun. 15, 7302 (2024)
    • J. Korean Phys. Soc. 88, 1094 (2026)
    • Phys. Rev. Applied 11, 034065 (2019)

  • Spin caloritronics

    Heat travels through magnetic insulators as both magnons and phonons. We measure how the two fall out of equilibrium and how thermal magnons diffuse, as groundwork for energy-efficient spin devices.

    • Phys. Rev. B 103, 174432 (2021)
    • Phys. Rev. Lett. 117, 107202 (2016)

  • Van der Waals magnets New direction

    Layered van der Waals ferromagnets such as Fe3GaTe2 keep their magnetic order and perpendicular anisotropy above room temperature, which makes them attractive for compact spin devices. We measure their magnetization dynamics with out-of-plane ferromagnetic resonance and study how electric currents change their magnetic properties.

    • Adv. Mater. 37, 2414917 (2025)

Facilities

A microwave and magneto-optical laboratory, built from an empty room at CBNU since September 2025.

Microwave spectroscopy
RF sources and analysers for in-plane and out-of-plane ferromagnetic resonance and spin-wave transmission.
Lock-in detection
Lock-in amplifiers for low-noise electrical detection of magnetization dynamics.
Magneto-optical Kerr effect
Laser-based MOKE setup on the optical table for optical detection of magnetization.
Magnetic fields
Electromagnets on bipolar power supplies, with an in-house Arduino-controlled rotation stage for angle-resolved measurements.
2D vector electromagnet In design
Four-pole in-plane vector magnet for rotating fields without moving the sample.
Spin-wave devices
Custom microwave antennas, including 1 mm and 0.5 mm designs, for propagating spin waves in the Damon–Eshbach geometry.
Simulation
Micromagnetic simulations and electromagnetic field calculations for antenna and device design.
Cryogenic platform Being built
Magnon–superconductor experiments through the Chungcheong Open-Lab project.

Principal investigator. Assistant Professor, Department of Physics Education, Chungbuk National University

Kyongmo An founded M² Lab in March 2025. His work connects spin waves with sound and microwave photons: Brillouin light scattering of magnons and phonons during his Ph.D. at UT Austin, spin-wave devices as a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow at EPFL, phonon-mediated coupling of magnets at SPINTEC in Grenoble, and hybrid magnonics as a Sejong Science Fellow at KRISS.
